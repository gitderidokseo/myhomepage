Who these rates are for

  • Advisory fuel rates apply only to employees driving a company car. HMRC says they must not be used in any other circumstances.
  • They cover two things: your employer reimbursing fuel for business travel, and you repaying the cost of fuel used privately.
  • Driving your own car for work is different — that uses mileage allowance payments. Self-employed? Use the HMRC mileage allowance calculator instead.
  • Hybrid cars are treated as petrol or diesel cars.

Business fuel: paying at or below the rate

If your employer pays no more than the advisory rate for your car's engine size and fuel, there is no taxable profit and no Class 1A National Insurance. If they pay more, they need to show the fuel cost per mile really is higher; otherwise the excess is taxable and counts as earnings for Class 1 National Insurance.

Private fuel: avoiding the fuel benefit charge

If your employer pays for fuel you use privately, you can face a car fuel benefit charge. HMRC says there is no fuel benefit charge if all private mileage is correctly recorded and you repay at the advisory rate or higher. That makes an accurate split of business and private miles the whole point.

Both cases depend on a mileage log

Whether you claim business fuel or repay private fuel, the numbers come from your record of each journey: the date, start and end points, the reason and the miles. See what an HMRC mileage log should record.
