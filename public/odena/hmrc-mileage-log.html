HMRC mileage log: what records you need

Updated 30 October 2026 · For self-employed sole traders

If you claim business mileage on your Self Assessment return, HMRC can ask you to show where the figure came from. A mileage log is that proof. Here is what it should contain, how long to keep it, and a rate change that catches people out on the return due 31 January 2027.

What to record for each journey

HMRC does not publish an official mileage log form. In practice, a log that will stand up to questions records, for every business journey:

Field Example
Date 14 Oct 2026
Start and end point Home, Leeds → Client site, Wakefield
Reason for the trip Kitchen installation for J. Smith
Business miles 11.4
Vehicle Van, AB12 CDE

Odometer readings at the start and end of the tax year are worth noting too. They let you show what share of the vehicle's total miles were for business.

Which journeys count

  • Usually business: driving to clients, customers or temporary sites, collecting supplies, going to the bank or post office for the business.
  • Usually not: ordinary commuting between home and a regular place of work, and private trips — even if you take a work call on the way.
  • Mixed trips: if you stop at a client and then go shopping, only the business part counts.

Where your "regular place of work" is depends on how you work, and it is the most common grey area. If you are unsure, check with HMRC or an accountant.

The rate on the return due 31 January 2027

The flat rate for cars and vans rose from 45p to 55p for the first 10,000 miles, but only from 6 April 2026. The return you file by 31 January 2027 covers the 2025–26 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026), so it still uses the old rates:

Car or van 2025–26 return (due Jan 2027) 2026–27 return (due Jan 2028)
First 10,000 miles 45p 55p
After 10,000 miles 25p 25p

Motorcycles stay at 24p. Source: GOV.UK — simplified expenses for vehicles. To see what your miles are worth at either rate, use the HMRC mileage allowance calculator.

How long to keep it

GOV.UK says self-employed records must be kept for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year they relate to. A log for 2025–26 should therefore be kept until at least 31 January 2032. See GOV.UK — how long to keep your records.

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

Any of them can work, as long as the log is complete and written at the time rather than rebuilt from memory in January. Reconstructed logs tend to have round numbers, missing days and journeys that do not match your invoices — exactly what invites questions.

Digital records are also becoming the norm. Sole traders with qualifying income over £50,000 have had to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax since 6 April 2026, and the threshold falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028 (GOV.UK).
