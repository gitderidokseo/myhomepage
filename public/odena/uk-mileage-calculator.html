HMRC mileage rates by tax year

The rate for cars and vans stayed at 45p a mile for fifteen years, then rose to 55p from 6 April 2026. The same approved mileage rates apply to employees and, as flat rates, to the self-employed.

Tax year Car or van, first 10,000 miles Car or van, after 10,000 Motorcycle Bicycle (employees)
2026–27 55p 25p 24p 20p
2011–12 to 2025–26 45p 25p 24p 20p

Sources: GOV.UK — travel, mileage and fuel rates and simplified expenses for vehicles. The 10,000-mile threshold applies per tax year. Rates change — always check GOV.UK.

Employees: claiming mileage allowance relief

  • If your employer pays less than the approved rate (or nothing), you can claim tax relief on the difference. The calculator's claim figure is that difference.
  • If your employer pays more, the extra is taxable.
  • You cannot claim separately for fuel, electricity, road tax, MOTs or repairs — the rate covers them.
  • Ordinary commuting does not count, unless you are travelling to a temporary workplace.
  • If you fill in a Self Assessment return, claim through the return. Otherwise use HMRC's online claim.

Claiming for past years

Employees can claim for the current tax year and the 4 previous tax years. Right now that means 2022–23 onwards — earlier years are out of time. Every year before 2026–27 uses the 45p rate. Self-employed? Past years are handled through your Self Assessment return, so speak to an accountant before amending one.

Self-employed: simplified expenses

  • You cannot use flat rates for a vehicle you have already claimed capital allowances on, and once you use them for a vehicle you must keep using them for it.
  • Parking and tolls can be claimed on top of the flat rate. Fuel, insurance and repairs are already covered by it.
  • The saving shown is income tax only. Class 4 National Insurance on your profits may reduce too.
  • Driving a company car? Use the advisory fuel rates calculator instead.

To claim, you need a mileage log

For employee claims, HMRC asks for copies of your mileage logs showing the reason for every journey and the postcodes of its start and end points. Self-employed people should keep the same: date, start and end points, reason and miles. Writing this up at the end of the year from memory is where claims go wrong.
